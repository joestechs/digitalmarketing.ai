E-commerce & Affiliate

Affiliate Marketing

Affiliate marketing pays partners a commission for driving sales or leads they refer — a performance-only channel where you only pay for results, not exposure.

How affiliate tracking works

  1. An affiliate shares a unique tracked link (containing their affiliate ID).
  2. A visitor clicks it; a cookie is set recording that affiliate as the referrer, with a defined "cookie window" (e.g. 30 days).
  3. If the visitor converts within that window, the affiliate is credited and paid the agreed commission.

Common commission structures

ModelPaid on
CPS (Cost Per Sale)A percentage of the sale value — most common e-commerce model
CPL (Cost Per Lead)A qualifying lead (signup, form fill) regardless of eventual purchase
CPA (Cost Per Action)Any specific defined action (trial start, app install)
Flat/RecurringFixed amount, sometimes recurring monthly for subscription products
⚠ Compliance Note

Affiliate content must disclose the relationship clearly (e.g., "This post contains affiliate links — I may earn a commission at no extra cost to you") under regulations like FTC guidelines. This applies to blog posts, videos, and social captions alike.

Running a successful affiliate program (merchant side)

  • Offer a competitive, clearly-explained commission structure
  • Provide ready-made creative assets (banners, tracked links, product feeds) to reduce affiliate friction
  • Communicate regularly — top affiliates need new promotions/products to keep content fresh
  • Monitor for fraud: cookie-stuffing, incentivized/fake traffic, and self-referral abuse
🎯 Interview Corner

Q: How is affiliate marketing different from influencer marketing?
A: They overlap but aren't identical: influencer marketing is typically paid regardless of outcome (flat fee for a post), while affiliate marketing is inherently performance-based (paid only when a tracked action occurs). Many modern partnerships blend both — a base flat fee plus an affiliate commission on top.
