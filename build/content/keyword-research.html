Keyword Research

Keyword research is the process of finding what your audience actually types into search engines, how often, and how hard it is to rank for — so you write content that meets real demand instead of guessing.

The core metrics

MetricWhat it means
Search VolumeAverage monthly searches for a keyword (often shown as a range, not exact)
Keyword Difficulty (KD)An estimate (0–100) of how hard it is to rank on page 1, based on competing sites' authority
CPCWhat advertisers pay per click on that keyword in Google Ads — a proxy for commercial value
SERP featuresWhether the results page shows a featured snippet, People Also Ask, video carousel, etc.

Short-tail vs. long-tail keywords

  • Short-tail ("shoes") — huge volume, extremely competitive, vague intent.
  • Long-tail ("best waterproof trail running shoes for wide feet") — lower volume individually, much easier to rank, higher conversion intent. Long-tail keywords, in aggregate, make up the majority of all search traffic.

A practical keyword research workflow

  1. Seed list — brainstorm 10–20 broad topics your product/persona cares about.
  2. Expand — run each seed through a keyword tool (Ahrefs, Semrush, Google Keyword Planner, or the free Google Search Console + autocomplete) to surface hundreds of variations.
  3. Mine competitors — check which keywords competitor pages already rank for ("content gap" reports).
  4. Cluster by intent — group keywords that would realistically be satisfied by the same single page (avoid one-page-per-keyword; it causes internal competition, or "keyword cannibalization").
  5. Prioritize — score each cluster by (Volume × Relevance) ÷ Difficulty to sequence your content calendar.
keyword-scoring.js
// Simple keyword opportunity score you can compute in a spreadsheet or script
function opportunityScore(keyword) {
  const { volume, difficulty, relevance } = keyword; // relevance: 1-10 you assign manually
  const difficultyFactor = Math.max(difficulty, 1); // avoid divide-by-zero
  return ((volume * relevance) / difficultyFactor).toFixed(2);
}

const keywords = [
  { term: "digital marketing tools",      volume: 9900, difficulty: 62, relevance: 9 },
  { term: "free digital marketing tools", volume: 2400, difficulty: 31, relevance: 10 },
];

keywords
  .map(k => ({ ...k, score: opportunityScore(k) }))
  .sort((a, b) => b.score - a.score)
  .forEach(k => console.log(`${k.term} → score ${k.score}`));

🎯 Interview Corner

Q: How do you decide which keywords to target first with a limited content budget?
A: Prioritize by opportunity, not just volume: look for keywords with clear commercial or informational relevance to your persona, moderate-to-low difficulty relative to your site's current authority, and existing SERP weaknesses (thin or outdated top-ranking content you can outdo).
