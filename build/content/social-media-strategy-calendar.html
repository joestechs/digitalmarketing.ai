Social Media Strategy & Content Calendar

Consistent, planned posting outperforms sporadic bursts of content. A social strategy defines what you post, why, and how success is measured — before you open a scheduling tool.

Choosing platforms deliberately

The biggest strategic mistake is trying to be everywhere at once. Choose platforms where your actual persona spends time and where your content format naturally fits, then go deep rather than spreading thin across six platforms with mediocre presence on each.

Content pillar framework

Define 3-5 recurring content pillars so you never run out of ideas and stay on-brand. Example for a B2B SaaS company:

Posting cadence & timing

There's no universal "best time to post" — it depends entirely on when your specific audience is active. Use each platform's native analytics to find your own audience's active hours, then test consistency over volume: 3-4 genuinely good posts a week outperforms daily filler content.

Community management

  • Respond to comments and DMs promptly — algorithms reward accounts that drive real conversation.
  • Have a documented response protocol for negative comments/reviews: acknowledge, take detail-gathering offline, never argue publicly.
  • Proactively engage with your audience's content, not just your own posts — social is inherently two-way.
🎯 Interview Corner

Q: How would you build a social media content calendar from scratch for a brand-new brand with zero existing data?
A: Start with persona research and competitor content audits (what formats/pillars perform well in the niche), define 3-5 content pillars tied to funnel stages, set a realistic cadence you can sustain, and build in a review cadence (biweekly/monthly) to swap underperforming pillars once real data starts coming in.
