How social algorithms generally decide reach

Every platform differs in specifics, but most weigh a common set of signals:

  • Early engagement velocity — how quickly the first viewers like/comment/share/watch fully
  • Dwell time / watch time — how long people actually look at or watch a post
  • Relationship signals — whether someone regularly interacts with your account already
  • Content format preference — platforms actively promote formats they're pushing (e.g., Reels, Shorts)
  • Negative signals — "see less," reports, or quick scroll-past all suppress future reach

Organic vs. paid social

Organic reach on most platforms has declined significantly as algorithms prioritize keeping users on-platform and reserve premium placement for advertisers. A modern strategy typically treats organic as the community/brand-voice layer and pairs it with paid boosting of top-performing organic content ("dark posts" or "boosted posts") for actual reach at scale.

Engagement rate — the metric that matters more than followers

engagement-rate.js
function engagementRate(likes, comments, shares, followers) {
  const interactions = likes + comments + shares;
  return ((interactions / followers) * 100).toFixed(2) + "%";
}

engagementRate(420, 38, 12, 10000); // "4.70%"

A smaller, highly-engaged audience routinely outperforms a large, passive one for actual business outcomes — engagement rate correlates with algorithmic reach and with real audience trust.

🎯 Interview Corner

Q: A brand has 100K followers but almost no engagement. What would you investigate?
A: Check follower quality (bought/bot followers, irrelevant audience from a viral but off-brand moment), content-audience mismatch, posting consistency, and whether CTAs actually invite interaction rather than just broadcasting. Also check whether engagement is being measured against total followers vs. actual reach — reach is usually a fraction of follower count.
