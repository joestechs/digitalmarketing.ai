Buyer Personas & Market Research

You cannot write a good ad, blog post, or email to "everyone." A buyer persona is a semi-fictional profile of your ideal customer, built from real data, that keeps every piece of marketing aimed at an actual human.

What goes into a persona

CategoryQuestions to answer
DemographicsAge range, location, income band, job title/industry
GoalsWhat outcome are they trying to achieve?
Pain pointsWhat's currently blocking them or costing them time/money?
ObjectionsWhy would they hesitate to buy? (price, trust, switching cost)
Information dietWhere do they search, scroll, and get recommendations?
Buying triggersWhat event pushes them from "browsing" to "buying"?

Where the data actually comes from

  • Customer interviews — 5–10 short calls beat 500 survey responses for real language and objections.
  • Support tickets & sales call notes — the exact phrasing customers use for their problems (gold for ad copy and SEO titles).
  • Google Analytics / GA4 audience reports — actual demographics and interest categories of current visitors.
  • Competitor reviews — G2, Trustpilot, App Store reviews reveal what people love and hate about alternatives.
  • Keyword research — search volume and phrasing tell you what problem language people actually use.
  • Social listening — Reddit threads, niche Facebook groups, and forum posts surface unfiltered opinions.
⚠ Common Mistake

Building a persona purely from imagination ("Marketing Mary, 32, loves yoga") without a single real data point. If you can't cite where a persona detail came from, cut it — it's decoration, not research.

Market research beyond the persona

Persona work answers "who." You also need to answer "what's the landscape":

  • Competitive analysis — who ranks for your target keywords, what do their ads say, what's their pricing?
  • TAM/SAM/SOM sizing — Total Addressable Market, Serviceable Available Market, Serviceable Obtainable Market — used to judge if a niche is big enough to justify a campaign budget.
  • SWOT analysis — Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats — a quick strategic gut-check before a campaign launch.
🎯 Interview Corner

Q: How many personas should a company have?
A: As few as the product genuinely requires — usually 1–3. More than that and campaigns become unfocused. The right answer signals you understand personas exist to sharpen targeting, not to check a box.
