The metric that ties it all together: LTV:CAC ratio

ltv-cac.js
function ltvToCac(ltv, cac) {
  const ratio = ltv / cac;
  let verdict;
  if (ratio < 1)      verdict = "Losing money on every customer";
  else if (ratio < 3)  verdict = "Marginal — needs improvement";
  else if (ratio <= 5) verdict = "Healthy, sustainable growth";
  else                 verdict = "Possibly under-investing in growth";
  return { ratio: ratio.toFixed(2), verdict };
}

ltvToCac(900, 250); // { ratio: '3.60', verdict: 'Healthy, sustainable growth' }

A widely-cited rule of thumb is that a healthy SaaS/subscription business targets an LTV:CAC ratio of roughly 3:1 — high enough to be profitable and fund growth, but not so high that the company is likely under-investing in customer acquisition relative to available opportunity.

🎯 Interview Corner

Q: A campaign has an LTV:CAC ratio of 8:1. Is that good?
A: On the surface it looks excellent — very profitable per customer. But an unusually high ratio is often a sign of under-spending: the company could likely spend more on acquisition (accepting a lower but still healthy ratio) and grow faster while remaining profitable. The "right" answer weighs available growth capital and market size, not just the raw number.
