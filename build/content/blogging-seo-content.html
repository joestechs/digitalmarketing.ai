Blogging & SEO Content

Blog content sits at the intersection of content marketing and SEO — written to satisfy search intent while genuinely helping the reader, structured so both humans and search engines can navigate it easily.

A practical SEO blog post structure

structure.md
H1: Primary keyword, framed as a real title (not keyword-stuffed)
  Intro (2-3 sentences): state exactly what the reader will get, no fluff preamble
  H2: First subtopic (often "what is X" if informational intent)
  H2: Second subtopic
    H3: Supporting detail
  H2: Practical/actionable section (steps, checklist, template)
  H2: FAQ section (mine "People Also Ask" for real questions)
  Conclusion: summarize + one clear next step (internal link or CTA)

Writing for skimmers

Most readers scan before they commit to reading. Support that behavior instead of fighting it:

  • Short paragraphs (2-4 sentences)
  • Descriptive subheadings that make sense read on their own, like a table of contents
  • Bulleted/numbered lists for anything sequential or comparative
  • Bold key phrases sparingly to help eyes catch the important bits
  • A table of contents (with jump links) for posts over ~1,500 words

Featured snippets

Google often pulls a direct answer into a "position zero" box above the normal results. To win it, answer the query in one tight paragraph (40-60 words) directly under the relevant heading, and format list/table-based answers as actual HTML lists/tables rather than prose.

snippet-bait.html
<h2>What is a bounce rate?</h2>
<p>
  Bounce rate is the percentage of visitors who leave a website after
  viewing only one page, without taking any further action. A high
  bounce rate can signal irrelevant traffic, a slow page, or unclear
  content — but context matters: a blog post answering one specific
  question can have a high bounce rate and still be successful.
</p>

Content refresh: your highest-ROI SEO activity

Updating an existing, already-ranking article (new data, expanded sections, updated screenshots, fixed broken links) frequently produces faster ranking gains than publishing something brand new, since the page already has some accumulated authority and indexing history.

⚠ Common Mistake

Publishing and never revisiting. Set a recurring quarterly review of your top 20 traffic-driving posts — check for outdated info, broken links, and opportunities to expand thin sections.

🎯 Interview Corner

Q: How long should a blog post be for SEO?
A: There's no magic word count — Google ranks based on satisfying intent, not length. The right process is checking the current top-ranking pages for that keyword and matching or exceeding their depth of coverage, not hitting an arbitrary number.
