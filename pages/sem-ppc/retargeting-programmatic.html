SEM & Paid Ads

Retargeting & Programmatic Advertising

Most visitors don't convert on their first visit. Retargeting (remarketing) keeps your brand in front of people who already showed interest, while programmatic advertising automates buying ad space across the wider web.

How retargeting works

  1. A visitor lands on your site with a tracking pixel installed.
  2. The pixel drops a cookie (or uses server-side tracking) tagging that browser/user as having visited a specific page or taken a specific action.
  3. When that person browses other sites in the ad network, your ads are eligible to show to them specifically.

Common retargeting segments

SegmentMessage strategy
All site visitors (last 30 days)General brand awareness reinforcement
Product page viewers, no purchaseShow the exact product viewed + social proof
Cart abandonersUrgency/incentive: "Your cart is waiting" ± limited discount
Past purchasersCross-sell/upsell, loyalty offers
Email list (non-purchasers)Sync as a Custom Audience for consistent cross-channel messaging
💡 Tip

Use frequency capping (limiting how many times one person sees an ad per day/week). Uncapped retargeting quickly turns into "ad fatigue" — annoyance that actively damages brand perception instead of nudging a conversion.

Programmatic advertising basics

Programmatic buying uses automated, real-time bidding (RTB) auctions to purchase display, video, and native ad inventory across thousands of publisher sites in milliseconds — instead of a human negotiating placements site-by-site.

TermMeaning
DSP (Demand-Side Platform)Where advertisers buy inventory (e.g., Google Display & Video 360, The Trade Desk)
SSP (Supply-Side Platform)Where publishers sell their ad inventory
Ad ExchangeThe marketplace connecting DSPs and SSPs via real-time auctions
RTB (Real-Time Bidding)The auction happening in the ~100ms it takes a page to load

The cookie-deprivation shift

Third-party cookies — the traditional mechanism for cross-site retargeting — are being phased out across major browsers. The industry is shifting toward: first-party data (your own email/CRM lists), server-side/Conversions API tracking, contextual targeting (matching ads to page content instead of user history), and privacy-preserving cohort-based targeting APIs.

⚠ Common Mistake

Relying entirely on browser pixels for conversion tracking. Server-side tracking (e.g. Meta Conversions API, Google Enhanced Conversions) is increasingly necessary as ad blockers and privacy settings block a growing share of client-side pixels.

🎯 Interview Corner

Q: What's the difference between remarketing and programmatic advertising?
A: Remarketing is a targeting strategy (show ads to people who already interacted with you). Programmatic is a buying method (automated, auction-based purchase of ad inventory). You can run remarketing campaigns programmatically, but programmatic also includes buying inventory for entirely new audiences.
