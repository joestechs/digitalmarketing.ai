CRO & A/B Testing

Conversion Rate Optimization (CRO) is the systematic process of increasing the percentage of visitors who take a desired action — through structured hypothesis testing, not guesswork or "best practice" copy-pasting.

The CRO process

  1. Research — analytics (where do people drop off?), heatmaps/session recordings, user testing, on-site surveys.
  2. Hypothesize — "Because [data], we believe changing [element] will cause [effect] for [audience]."
  3. Prioritize — score hypotheses (e.g. the PIE or ICE framework) so you test the highest-impact ideas first.
  4. Test — run a controlled A/B or multivariate test.
  5. Analyze & implement — ship the winner (or a partial learning) and move to the next hypothesis.

ICE prioritization framework

FactorQuestion
ImpactIf this wins, how much will it move the metric?
ConfidenceHow sure are we, based on data/precedent, that it will work?
EaseHow much effort/time to implement and test?

Score each 1-10 and multiply (or average) to rank your test backlog objectively instead of testing whatever a stakeholder liked most recently.

A/B testing statistics, simplified

ab-test-significance.js
// Simplified two-proportion z-test for A/B test significance
function isSignificant(convA, visitorsA, convB, visitorsB) {
  const pA = convA / visitorsA;
  const pB = convB / visitorsB;
  const pPooled = (convA + convB) / (visitorsA + visitorsB);
  const se = Math.sqrt(pPooled * (1 - pPooled) * (1 / visitorsA + 1 / visitorsB));
  const z = (pB - pA) / se;
  return { zScore: z.toFixed(2), significant: Math.abs(z) > 1.96 }; // ~95% confidence
}

isSignificant(120, 4000, 150, 4000);
// { zScore: '1.97', significant: true }
⚠ Common Mistake

Stopping a test early the moment it looks significant ("peeking"). Statistical significance calculated before reaching your pre-determined sample size is unreliable and inflates false positives. Decide sample size/duration in advance and stick to it.

What to test, roughly in order of typical impact

ElementWhy it tends to move the needle
Headline / value propositionDetermines whether the visitor understands the offer at all
CTA copy & placementDirectly gates the conversion action
Page/form length & frictionEvery extra step loses a percentage of visitors
Social proof placementReduces perceived risk right before the decision point
Pricing presentationAnchoring and framing measurably affect purchase decisions
Button colorUsually minimal impact alone — rarely worth testing in isolation

Qualitative research tools

  • Heatmaps (Hotjar, Microsoft Clarity) — visualize where people click, scroll, and stop.
  • Session recordings — watch anonymized real user sessions to spot confusion or friction.
  • On-site surveys/polls — ask "What almost stopped you from purchasing today?" directly.
🎯 Interview Corner

Q: A test shows a 15% lift but only ran for 3 days with 200 visitors per variant. Do you ship it?
A: No — that sample is almost certainly too small and the duration too short to account for day-of-week variance and reach statistical significance. Explain the risk of false positives from underpowered tests and recommend running it to a pre-calculated sample size across at least one full week (ideally 2+) before deciding.
