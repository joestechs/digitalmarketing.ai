Marketing Metrics & Formulas

The complete reference sheet of formulas every digital marketer is expected to know cold — these come up in almost every interview and every performance report.

CAC
Spend ÷ New Customers
LTV
AOV × Purchase Freq × Lifespan
ROAS
Revenue ÷ Ad Spend
CR
Conversions ÷ Visitors

Acquisition & spend metrics

MetricFormulaInterpretation
CPC — Cost Per ClickTotal Spend ÷ ClicksWhat one click costs
CPM — Cost Per Mille(Total Spend ÷ Impressions) × 1000Cost per 1,000 impressions — awareness efficiency
CPA — Cost Per AcquisitionTotal Spend ÷ ConversionsWhat one conversion (lead/sale) costs
CAC — Customer Acquisition CostTotal Sales & Marketing Spend ÷ New CustomersFully-loaded cost to win one customer

Performance & value metrics

MetricFormulaInterpretation
CTR — Click-Through RateClicks ÷ Impressions × 100Ad/content relevance & appeal
CR — Conversion RateConversions ÷ Total Visitors × 100How well traffic turns into outcomes
ROAS — Return on Ad SpendRevenue ÷ Ad Spend3.0 ROAS = $3 revenue per $1 spent
ROI — Return on Investment(Revenue − Cost) ÷ Cost × 100Net profitability including all costs, not just ad spend
AOV — Average Order ValueTotal Revenue ÷ Number of OrdersHow much a typical order is worth
LTV / CLV — Customer Lifetime ValueAOV × Purchase Frequency × Customer LifespanTotal value a customer generates over the relationship

The metric that ties it all together: LTV:CAC ratio

ltv-cac.js
function ltvToCac(ltv, cac) {
  const ratio = ltv / cac;
  let verdict;
  if (ratio < 1)      verdict = "Losing money on every customer";
  else if (ratio < 3)  verdict = "Marginal — needs improvement";
  else if (ratio <= 5) verdict = "Healthy, sustainable growth";
  else                 verdict = "Possibly under-investing in growth";
  return { ratio: ratio.toFixed(2), verdict };
}

ltvToCac(900, 250); // { ratio: '3.60', verdict: 'Healthy, sustainable growth' }

A widely-cited rule of thumb is that a healthy SaaS/subscription business targets an LTV:CAC ratio of roughly 3:1 — high enough to be profitable and fund growth, but not so high that the company is likely under-investing in customer acquisition relative to available opportunity.

Engagement & retention metrics

MetricFormula
Bounce RateSingle-page sessions ÷ Total sessions × 100
Churn RateCustomers lost in period ÷ Customers at start of period × 100
Retention Rate100% − Churn Rate
NPS (Net Promoter Score)% Promoters (9–10 rating) − % Detractors (0–6 rating)
🎯 Interview Corner

Q: A campaign has an LTV:CAC ratio of 8:1. Is that good?
A: On the surface it looks excellent — very profitable per customer. But an unusually high ratio is often a sign of under-spending: the company could likely spend more on acquisition (accepting a lower but still healthy ratio) and grow faster while remaining profitable. The "right" answer weighs available growth capital and market size, not just the raw number.
